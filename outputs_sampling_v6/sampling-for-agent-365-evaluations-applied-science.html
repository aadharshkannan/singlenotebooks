Speaker notes. Random is the control: use a SHA-256 seeded random priority without replacement, then admit whole sessions under the exact token budget. Variable token costs mean equal random priority is the precise property; labels and concepts never affect membership. MinHash is an adaptive lexical-novelty design. It canonicalizes bounded session evidence, creates field-tagged token trigrams, builds a 128-value MinHash signature, and uses 32 bands of 4 rows for per-agent candidate lookup. A bucket collision creates candidates; a signature similarity of at least 0.55 joins the best cluster. A bucket miss creates a new cluster with novelty 1.0 instead of triggering an exhaustive scan. It is fast and bounded, but lexical rather than semantic.

Evaluation objectives

Success means accuracy and breadth

Outcome-rate MAE

How far is the sample’s overall pass-rate estimate from the known population answer?

Accuracy of the headline rateLOWER IS BETTER

Concept coverage

How much of the population’s behavioral variety did the selected sessions touch?

Breadth across behaviorsHIGHER IS BETTER
Speaker notes. These metrics answer different questions. Selected-only outcome-rate MAE compares the pass rate calculated from selected sessions with the known census pass rate over all eligible sessions: `abs(selected_rate - census_rate)`. In the illustration, 69% versus 72% produces a three-percentage-point error; lower is better. Concept coverage counts distinct retrospective concept keys represented by at least one selected session, divided by all population concept keys. The experiment key is `corpus | domain | task | difficulty`. The 12 islands are a scaled visual analogy: touching three regions means 25%, corresponding to 120 of 480 concepts. Coverage counts whether a concept is touched, not how many sessions were selected from it. A method can reproduce the overall pass rate while missing rare concepts, or cover many concepts while shifting the headline rate; slide 7 shows that tradeoff.

Default External3p session-completion path
Speaker notes. Determinism comes from normalized identity plus pure hash arithmetic, not shared mutable state. Normalize IDs with trim and uppercase before hashing. Compute bucket from the first 32 bits of SHA-256 as an unsigned integer, then mod 100. Inclusion uses bucket < effective_rate where floors/caps are applied after deterministic computation. Positive TPM bypass remains an explicit operational route and is orthogonal to hash inclusion. This avoids legacy `HashCode.Combine` instability across runtime/process boundaries and keeps retries and reprocessing identical.

Speaker notes. This is a controlled comparison. Both sides hold the IDW estimate at 65 percent and use the same response-surface slope L. Only the weighted donor angle changes. Near donors contribute a 5-point geometric allowance, producing 60 to 70 percent. Far donors contribute a 20-point allowance, producing 45 to 85 percent. Both ranges use the same zero-to-100 scale, so their widths are directly comparable. The numbers are illustrative: the mechanism is `u_Lip = L × weighted donor angle`, with estimate ± u_Lip clamped to [0,1]. This is conditional geometric sensitivity, not a confidence interval, and it does not capture judge bias, embedding drift, or selection bias.

Question: can storage-safe representations preserve end-to-end behavior?

Reducer fit once (seed 13); 30 seeds vary replay, not the fit.
Speaker notes. This slide frames experiment design only, not results. The blocker is storage/compliance for raw 1536-dimensional vectors. We evaluate reducers (PCA, SVD, GRP, coordinate-mean baseline) across 1024 to 2 dimensions using the same end-to-end pipeline: reduce, select, estimate, compare to known labels. Metric is end-to-end error, not vector reconstruction fidelity. Dataset is transductive over full unlabeled population; t-SNE is intentionally omitted because it is visualization-only and non-invertible. Totals: 2,500 synthetic sessions, 30 replay seeds, 5 traffic schedules, 5 judge budgets, yielding 54,750 evaluation rows. No runtime benchmark claim is made here.
